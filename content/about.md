## Why This Blog Exists

I'm Tiberiu Petre — Software Engineer. The homepage has the résumé version of that; this page is closer to the why.

This is where I write down what I build, break, and figure out: project retrospectives, things I'm learning, decisions I made and would maybe make differently now. If a project, an idea, or a mistake taught me something worth keeping, it probably ends up here.

## How I Think, Organize, and Write

Most of what ends up here starts somewhere else: a running personal knowledge base where I dump notes, half-formed ideas, and technical detail as I go, then periodically reorganize into something coherent. Claude Code is the tool I use to work that base. It helps retrieving the relevant context, structuring it, and drafting the prose for an article from it.

I stay in the loop through all of it. I read every draft, correct it, argue with it when it's wrong, and decide what actually gets published. That's a different relationship than spell-check on something I typed first. Most of the wording here starts AI-drafted, not human-typed-then-polished.

What I hold the line on is the underlying facts. Code samples, technical claims, and project history are checked against real sources: repositories, READMEs, git history, my own memory when nothing else survives; they are not invented to fill a gap. When something can't be verified, the article says so directly instead of smoothing it into false confidence. If you read a line like "this is a reconstruction from memory, unverified," that hedge is deliberate, not filler.

The publishing side of this is its own small system: a draft branch, CI, cherry-picked commits onto a separate public branch, and a deploy pipeline. The infrastructure isn't really the point, though it occasionally becomes its own article. The point is that I've built a process for turning accumulated thinking into something publishable, and AI is one part of that process, not a replacement for the judgment at either end of it.

## About Me

I'm Tiberiu Petre, a gameplay programmer with four years of professional experience on Assassin's Creed, at Ubisoft Romania. I wrote about it in [Four Years at Ubisoft](/posts/four-years-at-ubisoft/): a migration that stalled, a mechanic called Pressure Plates, and what both taught me about the conditions I actually work well under. The homepage has the résumé version of that; this page is closer to the why.

Outside of writing code, I like to play video games. I usually go for story-driven games: Assassin's Creed, Horizon Forbidden West, Life is Strange, NieR Automata, the Tomb Raider Trilogy, Alan Wake II, Control, Cyberpunk 2077, Diablo and Overwatch (since 2017) are my cup of tea. What I like about these games is the story, the quest lines and interactions between the player and the world, alone or in a team.

When I started playing Overwatch, the hook was the story, before it started to go sideways. But multiplayer games are really about the friends you make along the way, and the fun of getting together for just one match before you realize you've lost the entire evening. That is the part I liked most about Overwatch. In theory, at least. Ranked has a way of testing those friendships.

On this blog I write about what I build, break, and figure out: project retrospectives, things I'm learning, decisions I made and would maybe make differently now, and sometimes what I play. If a project, an idea, or a mistake taught me something worth keeping, it probably ends up here.

## How I Think, Organize, and Write

Most of what ends up here starts somewhere else: a running personal knowledge base where I dump notes, half-formed ideas, and technical detail as I go, then periodically reorganize into something coherent. AI helps me retrieve the relevant context from it, structure it, and draft the prose for an article.

I stay in the loop through all of it. I read every draft, correct it, argue with it when it's wrong, and decide what actually gets published. That's a different relationship than spell-check on something I typed first. Most of the wording here starts AI-drafted, not human-typed-then-polished.

What I hold the line on is the underlying facts. Code samples, technical claims, and project history are checked against real sources: repositories, READMEs, git history, my own memory when nothing else survives; they are not invented to fill a gap. When something can't be verified, the article says so directly instead of smoothing it into false confidence. If you read a line like "this is a reconstruction from memory, unverified," that hedge is deliberate, not filler.

The publishing side of this is its own small system: a draft becomes an article through Hugo, committed onto a public branch and automatically deployed. The infrastructure isn't really the point; the point is that I've built a process for turning accumulated thinking into something publishable, and AI is one part of that process, not a replacement for the judgment at either end of it.
